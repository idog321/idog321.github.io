Before you go offline

See it work.

Drawing a line

Each click adds a point on the trail and the length shows along the segment. Click an earlier point to insert the next one after it.

Measuring as you draw

A polygon round Inland Lake. The card shows the area and perimeter while you draw. Nothing is saved unless you press Save.

Editing a line

Drag a point onto the trail, delete one, or drag the faint dot between two points to add one there.

Changing the draw order

Drag the Maps folder above the trail and the pins disappear under the rasters. Drag it back and they return.

Downloading tiles

Download Map Tiles from the Add Data menu, set the zoom level, and the tiles come down. Turn the streamed layer off and the downloaded area stays.

Adding stops to a route

Type each stop and pick it from the results. The roads fill in as you go, with the distance and time on the totals line.

The elevation profile

Move along the chart and a marker follows the trail on the map. Drag across the chart to select a stretch.

Switching the Apple map

The last button in the sidebar steps through Hybrid, Satellite, Off and Standard under your layers.

Built for privacy,
from day one.

TopoKit was built without an account. Your projects live on your device, and in your own iCloud if you turn on sync. Nothing you record is public by default, because nothing goes anywhere by default.

  • What touches the network: Apple's basemap, search and directions.
  • Elevation and map data you ask for, when you download elevation or build a basemap.
  • Map servers you add yourself.
  • The App Store, to check what you own, and one small file on topokit.ca that says where the map data lives.
  • That's the whole list. Everything else stays on your device.

Made by a working GIS technician.

Hi, I'm Nikolay. I'm an outdoor enthusiast and GIS technician in British Columbia, Canada.

I built TopoKit because I wanted to take my data outdoors, and I couldn't find a mapping app that handled both raster and vector data, kept everything private, and didn't lock me into an expensive subscription. So I made one. You can buy TopoKit once and keep it, or pay monthly if you only need it for a season.

I use the app weekly: for data collection, backcountry trips on my own time, and any fieldwork that starts where cell service ends.

I'm constantly learning from my own experiences with TopoKit and always working to improve it. If you have an idea or something you'd like me to fix, I'm happy to look into it. Every message comes straight to me.
