Maps / Juan de Fuca Marine Trail

Juan de Fuca Marine Trail maps

By Nikolay Senilov · Version 2026.11

I made seven maps covering the Juan de Fuca Marine Trail from China Beach to Botanical Beach, with the kilometre posts, campsites, trailheads and tide limits marked along the way. Each map is at 1:25,000 and can be printed on US Letter paper or used as a GeoPDF in TopoKit, where you can see your GPS location without cell service. The complete 11-page booklet is free to download, print and share.

Download the PDF8.9 MB

The PDF includes a cover, an overview of the trail, seven detailed maps and two pages explaining the map data, limitations and sources.

Using the maps in TopoKit

You can import the entire PDF into TopoKit at once, rather than adding each map separately. TopoKit places the seven maps into a folder named after the PDF, with each map available as its own layer. If you're asked to reproject the maps during import, select All to process them together.

On iPhone, you can open the PDF in TopoKit from Files, Mail or Messages, or import it using Add Data → Add Raster Layer. On Mac, you can drag the PDF directly onto the map. Once the maps are imported, your iPhone can show your GPS position on them without an internet connection.

The seven maps

  1. JDF-01 China Beach trailhead to Mystic Beach km 0–4
  2. JDF-02 Magdalena Point to Bear Beach km 5–11
  3. JDF-03 Bear Beach to Chin Beach Closed km 11–20 BC Parks closed this section after a slope failure, as of October 6, 2026, and there's no alternate route. Current advisories
  4. JDF-04 Chin Beach to Sombrio Beach km 20–29
  5. JDF-05 Sombrio Beach to Little Kuitsche Creek km 29–35
  6. JDF-06 Parkinson Creek trailhead to Payzant Creek km 36–42
  7. JDF-07 Soule Creek to Botanical Beach trailhead km 42–47

What's on the maps

Each map is north-up at 1:25,000 when printed at actual size on US Letter paper, meaning 4 cm on the page represents 1 km on the ground. Along with the trail and its kilometre posts, the maps show campsites, trailheads, toilets, viewpoints, waterfalls, suspension bridges and the six tide limits identified by BC Parks. Each tide limit is labelled with the height the tide must be below for that section to be passed.

The maps use 10 m elevation contours over shaded relief, with elevation data from Natural Resources Canada's HRDEM lidar and MRDEM-30 models. They also include a 1 km UTM grid and an elevation profile showing the terrain along each section. The coastal shading extends to the mapped low-tide line and doesn't distinguish between sand and exposed rock, while white dashes along the red trail line indicate sections estimated to follow the beach.

BC Parks lists the trail as 47 km long, although the route shown on these maps measures 40.4 km, so the kilometre-post numbers are better used to identify locations than to calculate how far you've walked. The notes at the end of the PDF explain where the data came from and how I prepared the maps.

Before you go

These maps are unofficial and were made using publicly available geographic data. They haven't been checked in the field and aren't produced or endorsed by BC Parks or any other land manager. The Juan de Fuca Marine Trail is in the territory of the Pacheedaht First Nation.

Some sections of the trail are impassable at high tide, and the beach shading or white dashes on the maps shouldn't be used to decide whether a crossing is safe. Check the Port Renfrew tide predictions against the tide limits marked on the maps, keeping in mind that weather and waves can raise the water above predicted levels. From early November to mid-March, a phone connected to a US cell tower may also show a time one hour behind BC's local time.

Parts of the trail were rerouted in 2026 and may not match the mapped route, so follow the trail on the ground and check BC Parks for updated conditions. Camping requires a backcountry camping permit, and the BC Parks website has information about current regulations, fees and advisories. Always carry another reliable way to navigate rather than depending on these maps alone.
