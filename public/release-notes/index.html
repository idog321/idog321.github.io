Release notes

Every TopoKit release, newest first. The same notes appear in the app under Settings, in What’s New.

Version 1.2

TopoKit Is Now Free to Download

Try everything free for a month on the monthly plan, or unlock TopoKit forever with one purchase. Download it before January 1, 2027 and it's yours forever. If you already bought TopoKit, you own this update and every one after it on every iPhone and Mac signed into your Apple Account, with nothing to buy or restore.

Build Your Own Offline Basemaps

Pick an area and TopoKit builds a topo map of it right on your iPhone or Mac, with shaded relief, contours, slope angle, trails, roads, water and place names. Start with the Light or Dark look from the new Basemaps button, or set your own colours. Once it's built, it works with no signal.

Tap or Search the Map

Tap a trail, a peak or one of your own lines and a card tells you what it is. You can save trails and parks from your basemaps into your project, and search finds their names even with no signal. Hold your finger on any spot, or right-click it on a Mac, to see its height, slope, sunrise and sunset.

Elevation Profiles for Every Line

Open the elevation profile from any line's card, including recorded tracks and trails on your basemaps. Select part of the chart to see the climb for that stretch, and it lights up on the map. The profile asks before it downloads terrain, and gain and loss no longer count the small bumps in the terrain data.

Directions Is Now Add Route

Add stops by searching or tapping the map, and the roads between them fill in for driving, walking or cycling. You can check the elevation profile before saving, then share the route as a file or open it in another maps app.

Add and Remove Points

While you draw a line or an area, or edit a saved one's points, drag the small dot between any two points to add a new point there. To remove one, tap it twice and choose Delete, or right-click it on a Mac.

New Pins, and Which Way You're Facing

Points have a new pin that looks the same on iPhone and Mac, with its name underneath. Add a point from a peak on your basemap and it keeps the peak's name and height. On iPhone, your location dot is orange now, with a little nose that shows which way you're facing.

Rebuilt Settings and Safer Sync

Settings has been rebuilt to work like the settings in your other apps, and the Mac has a proper Settings window. Sync merges changes from your other devices instead of overwriting them, and you can restore any backup. You can also copy, paste and duplicate layers and folders, even between projects.

Fixes Across the Board

Lots of fixes on both iPhone and Mac. Imported maps line up more accurately, imports and exports keep more of your data, and it's much harder to lose your work.

Something Is Hiding

On iPhone, at the bottom of Settings. Keep pulling.

Version 1.1

Drawing and Measuring Are Now One Set of Tools

Add Line, Add Polygon, and the new Add Circle tool show live measurements while you draw — distance, area, radius, and more, with tappable unit switching. Just measuring? Read the numbers and close the tool — nothing is saved unless you press Save. The tools draw with your default styles from Settings.

Choose How Imported Files Look

Imported tracks and shapes keep their familiar neutral style, and Settings now has separate Imported Line and Imported Polygon styles — so the look of your imports is independent from the style used while drawing.

Much Faster with Big Projects

Importing large files is dramatically faster, the map stays smooth while you pan and zoom, and layers no longer rebuild from scratch when something changes. Project files are also about half their previous size, so they open quicker and sync less data.

Better Imported Maps

Very large maps now keep their full detail without running out of memory, misaligned georeferenced PDFs place correctly, several Canadian coordinate systems are fixed, and a crash when importing custom-projection maps is gone. Georeferenced PDFs also redraw far faster while zooming and panning — especially on iPhone with many map sheets loaded.

Smoother Startup

Your last project starts opening immediately instead of waiting on cloud storage — and reopens reliably even when the cloud is slow. The first seconds after a project opens have far less flicker.

Fixed: Opening Files from Other Apps

Choosing “Open in TopoKit” for a map or GPS file from the Files app (or another app) now imports it into your project instead of doing nothing.

Stability Fixes Across the Board

Style editors no longer open half-empty on iPhone, pop-ups no longer break the map, edit panels only save what you actually changed, and GPS files with bad values can no longer block a project from saving.

Version 1.0.2

Edited Point Coordinates Now Save

Typing new coordinates into a point and tapping Done now saves them, even if you didn't dismiss the keyboard first. Previously the edit was discarded silently and the point snapped back to its old location.

Tracks Now Keep Full GPS Data

Recorded tracks keep elevation, timestamps, and accuracy on every point — not just the start and end. Your speed, elevation gain, and timing data are preserved when you save the track.

GPX Exports Work With Other Apps

Tracks exported as GPX now include per-point elevation and time, so they import cleanly into other GPS apps. Previous exports were technically valid but missed information that other apps expect to see.

KML Tracks Use Google Earth Format

Recorded tracks export as proper Google Earth track elements with 3D positions and per-point timestamps, so they animate and display correctly when opened elsewhere.

GeoJSON Now Includes Elevation

GeoJSON exports include the third dimension on every coordinate when elevation data is available, plus per-point timestamps for tracks.

GeoPackage 3D Support

GeoPackage exports use 3D geometry when features have elevation, so QGIS and ArcGIS recognize the data without manual conversion.

Imports Preserve More Data

Tracks and waypoints imported from GPX, KML, GeoJSON, and GeoPackage files keep their elevation and timestamp information, so a round-trip through TopoKit no longer strips per-point data.

Tap-to-Reopen Toolbar Tab Fixed

When the side toolbar is hidden, the small tab on the screen edge is now reliably tappable in Glass appearance mode. Previously the rounded glass shape shrank the tap area and made the tab feel unresponsive.

Debug Mode for Reporting Problems

A new Developer & Diagnostics section in Settings lets you turn on Debug Mode. When something goes wrong, the app shows what happened and lets you email the details to us with one tap, so problems are much easier to track down and fix.

Crash Reporting

If the app closes unexpectedly, the next time you open it you'll be asked whether to send a report. Sharing it helps us find and fix the cause quickly. You can always decline.

Version 1.0.1

Faster Elevation Downloads

Elevation data downloads are faster and the progress bar now shows smooth progress while each tile downloads, instead of jumping only when a tile finishes.

Crash Recovery for Tracks

If the app closes unexpectedly during a recording, you can recover your track on next launch.

Offline Pinning

Projects pinned for offline now keep your rasters and photos in sync, not just the project file.

Recording Profiles Redesigned

New names, clearer accuracy thresholds, and a new Record All Fixes option for raw GPS data.

Better GPS Feedback

See right away when signal is weak or Precise Location is disabled.

More Tile Layers Supported

Works with more tile servers.

Stability Improvements

Better offline tile caching, crash fixes for vector file imports, and under-the-hood reliability work.
